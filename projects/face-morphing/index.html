Face Morphing and Historical Understanding

What viewers attend to when their own face appears in a film about the Nagasaki atomic bombing

Period
2025–2026
Links
  • Exhibition Site

As fewer people with direct memory of the Second World War remain, museums and archives look for ways to help people who did not witness it engage with that history. One approach puts the viewer's own face on a character in historical media. This project, with Shohei Komatsu and Prof. Hidenori Watanave, asks what that self-representation actually does: whether it brings viewers closer to the situation depicted, or mainly draws their attention to their own face and to the technology.

In a pilot reported at ADADA 2025, high-school students in a peace-education program had their faces composited, with the AVATARIUM Portable system by POCKET RD, into scenes before and after the bombing from the film Nagasaki: In the Shadow of the Flash, used with the production team's permission. They rated the experience positively, but their comments centred on their own faces and on enjoyment, and historically grounded empathy was rare. The paper proposes "progressive de-selfing" as a design direction and discusses the integrity of AI-generated historical content.

The same kind of experience was shown to the public at the exhibition Mirai no Heiwa Katsudo-ten in Nagasaki (Future Peace Activities Exhibition), held by the Watanave Lab and NIB Nagasaki International Television at the Nagasaki Atomic Bomb Museum from 11 to 17 August 2025. A follow-up study, now in progress, separates two design choices that the pilot left mixed together: showing the viewer's face, and guiding the viewer to imagine being in the place depicted.

Structure

  1. 01

    Pilot Study (ADADA 2025)

    2025

    Face morphing with adolescents viewing a Nagasaki atomic-bomb film

    A pilot workshop with 12 female high-school students (ages 15 to 17) in the peace-education program of Hiroshima Jogakuin High School. Using AVATARIUM Portable, their faces were composited into a pre-bombing scene, in pairs, and a post-bombing scene, alone, from the film Nagasaki: In the Shadow of the Flash, while they thought aloud and then rated the experience. Ratings were high (mean 6.15 on a 7-point scale), but comments focused mostly on their own faces and on fun. A few participants described viewing the scene as a protagonist.

  2. 02

    Follow-up Study (ongoing)

    2026

    Separating self-representation from self-situating guidance

    An ongoing study that treats personalization as two separate design choices. Self-representation morphs a recurring character's face toward the viewer's own. Self-situating guidance is a single sentence shown before a film that asks viewers to imagine being in the place depicted. Adult visitors to an exhibition in Nagasaki watch four short films about the atomic bombing under conditions that cross the two factors, and a second, exploratory study records conversations between pairs who watch together. Results have not yet been reported.
