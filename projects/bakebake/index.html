Project BAKEBAKE

Yokai folklore archives and generative AI

Period
2026
Threads
Digital Archive (Yokai)
Links
  • Project Site

Project BAKEBAKE approaches yokai folklore from two directions. The first is computational analysis of the Database of Folktales of Mysterious Phenomena and Yokai, maintained by the International Research Center for Japanese Studies (Nichibunken), which holds more than 35,000 records: how the records are distributed, where they are biased, and which types recur. The second is an apparatus that uses yokai as an interface through which people today can put an unexplained experience or a vague unease into words, give it a name and a form, and look at it from outside.

On the archive side, the team represents the places mentioned in short folklore summaries without claiming more spatial precision than the records support; this work was published in the ISPRS Archives for the FOSS4G 2026 Academic Track. On the generation side, a visitor's account is matched against the database, generative models propose candidate names, narratives, and images, and the visitor chooses, edits, or rejects them before the result is printed on thermal paper. The apparatus was exhibited at YOKAI EXPO 2026 on Shodoshima from February to May 2026 and is reported at HAI 2026 and in SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 Art Papers.

The team (Kosuke Shimizu, Hiroki Ichikura, Riri Ikebe, Mirai Hoshikawa) is based at the University of Tsukuba and Japan Women's University and is supported by the Nippon Foundation HUMAI Program. Kosuke Shimizu implements the apparatus and designs the experiments.

Structure

  1. 01

    Yokai Geography

    2026

    Resolution-aware representation of place evidence in a yokai archive

    Folklore records often give only a prefecture, and their summaries mix place names with descriptions of rivers, roads, or graves, so plotting a representative point overstates precision. For 33,378 records from the Nichibunken yokai database, we built a representation that keeps administrative support areas, candidate toponyms, non-toponymic place descriptions, and provenance separate from display anchors. Rule-based matching assigned municipality support to 1,231 records, shrinking support area by a median 97.5% relative to the prefecture. A distance test did not show Kappa records lying closer to mapped major water.

  2. 02

    BAKEBAKE XR Generator

    2026

    An installation that turns an unexplained experience into a yokai

    T2Interfaces for Reconnecting with Memory

    An installation that compresses the naming of a yokai into a few minutes. A visitor describes an unexplained experience; the system retrieves similar records from the Nichibunken database and proposes candidate names, short narratives, and an image in one of five pictorial styles, which the visitor accepts, edits, or replaces. The entry is printed on thermal paper that fades. At YOKAI EXPO 2026 on Shodoshima, some entries named workplace and infrastructure unease with no precedent in the database.
