Speech Emphasis

Finding the words a speaker stresses, then showing them in captions and letting listeners feel them

Period
2026
Threads
T1Somatosensory Stimulation InterfacesT3Computational Systems for Human Capability

When people speak, they make some words stand out by lengthening them, saying them louder, or moving their pitch. Linguists call this prominence. Captions and transcripts lose it, because every word looks the same on screen. This project studies which acoustic cues make a word stand out, builds detectors light enough to run in a browser in real time, and passes the result on to listeners in two forms.

On the modeling side, an ICSV32 paper predicted human emphasis labels and labels computed with a continuous wavelet transform (CWT) on the same words, and found that the two do not support the same conclusions about which cues matter. An Interspeech 2026 paper introduced the Auditory Contrast Network, a model with at most 238 parameters that judges each word against its neighbours from duration, energy and spectral cues, without using text.

On the presentation side, detected emphasis sets the size or motion of words in captions, in three browser demos: live captions from a microphone, a player for TED talk excerpts, and a playground for moving type. In Visceral Resonance, the same signal drives electrical muscle stimulation on the listener's abdomen; it was shown as a demo at Augmented Humans 2026.

Structure

  1. 01

    Emphasis Captions

    2026

    Captions that carry how words are spoken, not only what is said

    T3Computational Systems for Human Capability

    Ordinary captions show what was said but not which words the speaker stressed. These three demos map detected emphasis onto the caption itself: word size in live microphone captions, size and weight over TED talk excerpts, and syllable motion in a playground for moving type. All run in the browser, and the emphasis is estimated from the sound of the voice.

    1. 01.1

      Live Captions

      2026

      Microphone captions in which stressed words appear larger

      Speech from the microphone is transcribed by the browser's Web Speech API, or optionally by Google Cloud Speech-to-Text through a small server, while a C prominence detector compiled to WebAssembly scores the same audio. Each word is shown in one of three sizes following WCAG-based 12/18/24 pt steps, so stressed words appear larger. A few seconds of silence set the noise floor, and reading a sample sentence adapts the thresholds to the speaker's voice.

    2. 01.2

      TED Talk Player

      2026

      TED talk excerpts with four subtitle styles to compare

      Ninety-second excerpts from six TED talks, three with strongly marked emphasis and three with flatter delivery, play with word-aligned subtitles. Four subtitle modes can be switched: uniform text, sizes by part of speech, sizes and weights from Auditory Contrast Network prominence scores, and a combination of part of speech and prominence.

    3. 01.3

      Moving Captions

      2026

      A playground where stressed syllables move, driven by sound alone

      Prosody Playground animates captions on TED excerpts syllable by syllable, using signal processing only and no pronunciation dictionary. Syllable nuclei, onset sharpness, noisiness and nucleus length are measured from the band-passed energy envelope, spectral flux and zero-crossing rate, and a syllable moves only when it stands out from the surrounding 1.5 seconds. Presets switch between static, weight-based and onomatopoeia-like motion, and a timeline shows prominence and pitch for each clip.

  2. 02

    Visceral Resonance

    2026

    Feeling a speaker's emphasis as abdominal muscle stimulation

    T1Somatosensory Stimulation Interfaces

    Visceral Resonance detects emphasis in live speech and delivers it to the listener's abdomen as electrical muscle stimulation (EMS) from a commercial audio-driven device. The working hypothesis is that contracting the abdominal muscles changes intra-abdominal pressure and may contribute to gut-like sensations. It was shown as a demo at Augmented Humans 2026; a sole-authored workshop paper at the same conference discusses how much freedom such modality translation should leave open, with a focus on agency.

  3. 03

    Modeling Emphasis

    2026

    Which acoustic cues make a word sound prominent?

    Two papers ask what in the sound makes a word stand out. At ICSV32, human and CWT-derived emphasis labels on the same words were predicted from the same cues; which cues helped beyond duration depended on the label. At Interspeech 2026, the Auditory Contrast Network, with three acoustic features and at most 238 parameters, matched a 94.6M-parameter wav2vec2 baseline in cross-corpus transfer (r = 0.412 vs 0.409), with the cue order duration > energy > spectral ≫ F0.
